Key Themes and Trends: 
	Group literature by topics, themes, or chronological developments to identify patterns.

Research Gaps and Limitations: 
	Identify unexplored areas, conflicting findings, or methodological weaknesses in previous studies.

Methodological Approaches: 
	Document how previous research was conducted (e.g., qualitative, quantitative, specific techniques) to identify best practices or gaps in methods.

Theoretical Frameworks: 
	Identify the core theories, models, or definitions foundational to the research topic.

Significant Findings/Conclusions: 
	Record the main results of seminal and current studies, highlighting consensus and debates.

Conceptual Definitions: 
	Note how key terms are defined and used across different sources.


Focus of the Extraction:
Critical Evaluation: 
	Rather than summarizing, evaluate the strength of evidence and reliability of findings.
Synthesis: 
	Connect findings from multiple studies to create a cohesive argument.
Relevance: 
	Focus on studies that directly support, challenge, or contextualize your research problem.
